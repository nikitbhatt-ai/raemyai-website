Who this is for

An AI strategy engagement is the right starting point for businesses where the operational pattern is more complex than a single obvious workflow problem. Specifically:

  • Multi-location or multi-provider operations where automation decisions affect multiple stakeholders and the right sequencing matters more than the individual workflow choice.
  • Businesses with multiple competing priorities — a medical practice debating between EHR replacement, AI scribe deployment, and operational automation, for example. Strategy work prioritizes correctly so capital and team capacity are not split.
  • Businesses recovering from a failed AI rollout where the team is skeptical, the technology stack is fragmented, and the second attempt cannot afford to fail. Strategy work resets the foundation cleanly.
  • Businesses preparing for a transaction — sale, merger, capital raise — where AI infrastructure is part of the diligence story and needs to be coherent.

For most independent businesses with one obvious operational pain point — slow lead response, high no-show rate, missed inbound calls — strategy work is unnecessary. Go straight to AI implementation and capture the value faster.

How the engagement runs

Week 1

Kickoff and stakeholder interviews

Initial alignment with founder/owner. Interviews with 4-8 operational stakeholders — practice manager, dispatcher, intake coordinator, GM, depending on the business. We establish what is actually happening today, not what the org chart says is happening.

Weeks 2–3

Workflow mapping and quantification

Every operational workflow documented. Time spent quantified. Revenue impact estimated. System inventory captured. Where the leakage is, what it costs, and which workflows are the right targets.

Week 4

Roadmap construction

Prioritized 12-month roadmap built. Workflows sequenced by ROI per setup hour, dependency mapped, vendor recommendations finalized, risk assessment completed. Draft deliverable shared for stakeholder review.

Weeks 5–6

Delivery and handoff

Final Operations Blueprint delivered. Live presentation to the leadership team. Implementation handoff plan reviewed. Whether you implement with Raemy AI, with another vendor, or in-house, you leave the engagement with everything you need to execute.

What this costs and how to credit it

AI strategy engagements typically range from $8,000 to $20,000 depending on the scope of operational mapping and number of stakeholders. For most independent businesses with 5-30 staff, the engagement lands at $10,000-$15,000.

The full engagement fee is creditable against a subsequent implementation engagement with Raemy AI within 12 months of strategy delivery. So if the strategy work leads to an implementation engagement — which it does about 70% of the time — the strategy work effectively becomes the discovery phase of the implementation, with no double-counting on cost.

If you choose to implement with another vendor or in-house, the strategy work still belongs to you. The Operations Blueprint is your document.

Frequently asked questions

What is included in an AI strategy engagement?

Operational discovery and workflow mapping, quantified opportunity analysis per workflow, prioritized 12-month roadmap, vendor and tool recommendations, written deliverable and presentation, and one quarterly review session.

How long does an AI strategy engagement take?

4 to 6 weeks from kickoff to written roadmap delivery, depending on business complexity and stakeholder availability.

How much does AI strategy cost?

Typically $8,000 to $20,000 depending on scope. The full fee is creditable against a subsequent implementation engagement within 12 months.

Do I need a strategy engagement before implementation?

For most independent businesses with one obvious operational pain point, no — go straight to implementation. Strategy engagements add the most value when the business has multiple competing priorities, complex stakeholder dynamics, or has previously failed an AI rollout and needs a clean second attempt.

Will you recommend competitors if they're a better fit?

Yes. The Operations Blueprint includes vendor recommendations, and if the right answer is another vendor — or in-house build — that is what we put in writing. The trust we build with strategy clients comes from the engagements where we recommend something other than ourselves.
