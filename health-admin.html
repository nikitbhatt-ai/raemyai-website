Who this is for

Health admin is not the same buyer as the clinical practice owner. It is the operation behind the practice — the people responsible for revenue cycle, claims, eligibility, prior authorization, and the operational reporting that practice leadership actually uses to make decisions. Specifically:

  • Independent revenue cycle management (RCM) firms processing 500-5,000+ monthly prior authorizations across multiple practice clients. The workflow burden compounds with scale, and the firms that automate first systematically out-margin the ones that do not.
  • Medical billing companies serving small and independent practices. The economics of billing companies depend on staff productivity per account; automation directly improves the unit economics.
  • Multi-practice billing operations — DSOs, dental groups, multi-location med spas, regional functional medicine groups with centralized billing. The cross-practice reporting and standardized workflows are the highest-ROI targets.
  • Practice managers and administrators who functionally are the back office for one or more practices. The role exists because the work is too operationally complex for the practice owner to handle directly. AI takes the complexity down without reducing the role's value.

If you are the clinical practice owner (functional medicine, med spa, integrative practice, etc.) and not the back-office buyer, the right starting point is Raemy Health & Wellness, not this page. Many operations use both — the practice owner buys clinical workflow automation, and the RCM firm buys back-office workflow automation. They complement each other.

The Five Workflows We Automate First

Health admin engagements have a different workflow library than clinical engagements. The five we deploy first cover the highest-volume, highest-revenue-impact functions of the back-office.

Workflow 01

Prior authorization tracking at scale

RCM firms processing hundreds or thousands of monthly authorizations face a different problem than a single practice. The workflow has to scale across multiple payers, multiple practices, multiple procedure types, and multiple specialists handling different parts of the queue. Our implementation tracks every authorization across all dimensions with payer-specific aging thresholds, automated follow-up triggers, and surface-of-truth dashboards per specialist. Average reduction in handling time: 60-80%.

Workflow 02

Denial management & appeal automation

When a denial comes in, the system parses the denial reason code, classifies the denial type, pulls the original authorization or claim data, and auto-drafts the appeal letter with the relevant supporting documentation. A billing specialist reviews and submits in under 5 minutes instead of reconstructing the appeal from scratch. Average appeal turnaround drops from 14-21 days to under 72 hours. Recovered revenue from previously-missed appeal deadlines typically lands at 12-25% of denied claim value.

Workflow 03

Batch eligibility verification

Eligibility verification done one-at-a-time consumes 5-10 minutes per patient. At scale across hundreds of weekly appointments, that is 25-50 staff hours per week. Our batch verification workflow runs against scheduled appointments overnight, flags eligibility gaps before patients arrive, and surfaces issues by 7 AM in a clean dashboard. Front-desk and billing teams stop chasing eligibility on the day of service.

Workflow 04

A/R aging workflows

Aged accounts receivable is the silent killer of billing operation cash flow. Our A/R workflow surfaces every outstanding claim with aging indicators by payer (green under 30 days, yellow 30-60, red over 60), routes follow-up tasks automatically based on payer-specific behavior patterns, and triggers patient balance sequences in parallel. Average improvement in A/R turns: 15-30% within the first 90 days.

Workflow 05

Payer-specific performance reporting

Most billing operations report at the aggregate level — total A/R, total claims, total collections. The leverage is at the payer level — which payers deny most often, which procedures get rejected, where authorization delays concentrate, where the work is leaking. Our reporting workflow surfaces this by payer, by procedure, by practice, and by individual specialist. Decisions that previously took weeks of data work happen in real time.

How this differs from Raemy Health & Wellness

Raemy Health (the clinical practice product) and Raemy Health Admin (the back-office product) share infrastructure but serve different buyers and address different workflows. The honest comparison:

  • Buyer: Health = practice owner / physician founder. Health Admin = RCM owner, billing company leadership, practice administrator.
  • Workflow library: Health = appointment reminders, no-show recovery, new patient intake, basic insurance verification, billing follow-up. Health Admin = prior auth at scale, denial management, batch eligibility, A/R aging, payer-specific reporting.
  • Scale: Health = single practice, 1-6 providers. Health Admin = multi-practice or multi-client volume, often 500+ monthly transactions per workflow.
  • PHI exposure: Health is built with a clear PHI boundary so many workflows run on operational data without touching PHI. Health Admin necessarily touches PHI more directly — appeal letters, eligibility checks, denial reasons. We deploy on HIPAA-compliant infrastructure with a BAA from day one.
  • Pricing: Health = $25K-$80K year one. Health Admin = $40K-$150K year one. The cost difference reflects scale, integration depth, and ongoing operation complexity.

Many operations use both products in parallel. A multi-practice group's clinical sites run Raemy Health for patient-facing workflows; their centralized billing operation runs Raemy Health Admin for back-office workflows. The same underlying infrastructure powers both.

Implementation path

Health admin engagements typically take 60-90 days from kickoff to full implementation. The structure:

  • Days 0-14: Discovery — payer mix analysis, workflow mapping, current-state operational audit, BAA execution.
  • Days 15-45: First two workflows live — typically prior auth tracking and denial management, because the ROI lift is fastest and most measurable.
  • Days 45-75: Eligibility verification batches and A/R aging workflows added. Most engagements break even on cost at this stage.
  • Days 75-90: Payer-specific performance reporting live. Full operational dashboard handed to your leadership team. ROI tracking continues.
  • Day 90+: Steady-state operation with monthly reporting and quarterly optimization. New workflows added as the operation identifies them — payer credentialing tracking, contract negotiation prep, fee schedule analysis, and others.
